🥘 Valencia Travel Guide

Overview

Valencia, Spain's third-largest city, offers a perfect blend of old and new. Known as the birthplace of paella, this Mediterranean coastal city combines historic architecture, futuristic design, beautiful beaches, and a laid-back lifestyle that makes it ideal for travelers seeking authentic Spain without the crowds of Barcelona or Madrid.

The city sits on Spain's eastern coast, blessed with 300 days of sunshine per year. Valencia successfully balances tradition with innovation — its old town preserves medieval charm while the City of Arts and Sciences showcases cutting-edge architecture.

💰 Budget Estimate

Valencia is more affordable than Madrid or Barcelona. A typical 3-day trip costs €140-190 per person:

  • Accommodation: €35-45 per night (budget hotel or hostel)
  • Food: €25-35 per day (including one good paella meal)
  • Transportation: €10-15 (metro/bus card or bike rental)
  • Attractions: €20-30 (City of Arts entry, museums)

🎨 Top Attractions

Modern Architecture

  • City of Arts and Sciences — Futuristic complex designed by Santiago Calatrava. Includes Oceanogràfic (Europe's largest aquarium, €32), Hemisfèric (IMAX cinema, €8), and Science Museum (€8). Combined tickets available.
  • Palau de les Arts — Stunning opera house, part of the same complex. Tours available €12.

Historic Center

  • Valencia Cathedral — Gothic cathedral claiming to house the Holy Grail. €8 entry, climb the Miguelete bell tower for city views.
  • La Lonja de la Seda — UNESCO-listed Gothic silk exchange, one of Spain's finest civil Gothic buildings. €2 entry (free Sundays).
  • Central Market (Mercado Central) — Stunning Art Nouveau market hall with fresh produce, meats, and seafood. Free to wander, great for breakfast.
  • Plaza de la Virgen — Beautiful central square with fountains, cathedral, and café terraces.
  • Torres de Serranos & Quart — Medieval city gates offering rooftop views. €2 entry (free Sundays).

Parks & Gardens

  • Turia Gardens — 9km park built in a drained riverbed, perfect for walking, cycling, or picnicking. Free.
  • Bioparc — Immersive zoo with African habitats. €27 entry (book online for discount).
  • Albufera Natural Park — Wetland lagoon south of Valencia, famous for bird watching and sunset boat rides (€4-6).

Beaches

  • Malvarrosa Beach — Main city beach, wide and sandy. Great for swimming and waterfront dining.
  • El Cabanyal — Former fishing neighborhood near beach, known for colorful tiled houses and local atmosphere.

🍽️ Food & Paella

Valencia is the birthplace of paella — here's how to eat it properly:

  • Traditional paella: Made with rabbit, chicken, and green beans (NOT seafood). Authentic paella is only served at lunchtime.
  • Where to eat paella:
    • La Pepica (Malvarrosa beach) — historic, touristy but good
    • Casa Roberto (near Central Market) — local favorite
    • Restaurante Levante (Albufera area) — authentic setting
  • Price: €12-18 per person (minimum 2 people). Beware of cheap paella — likely pre-made.
  • Other Valencian dishes: All i pebre (eel stew), fideuà (noodle paella), horchata with fartons (sweet drink with pastries).
  • Menú del día: €10-14 for 3-course lunch in local restaurants.
  • Central Market: Perfect for fresh juice, cheese, ham, and cheap breakfast (€3-5).

🚇 Getting Around

  • Metro & Bus: Clean, efficient system. Single ticket €1.50, 10-trip card €8.
  • Valencia Tourist Card: 24h (€15), 48h (€20), 72h (€25) — includes transport + museum discounts. Worth it if visiting multiple attractions.
  • Cycling: Valenbisi bike share (€13.30/week), or rent from shops (€10-12/day). City is flat and bike-friendly.
  • Walking: Old town is compact and walkable. Modern areas are farther but connected by metro.
  • From Airport: Metro lines 3 & 5 to center (€3.90, 25min), or bus 150 (€1.50).

📝 Travel Tips

  • ✔ Eat paella at lunchtime (1-3pm) — never at dinner. Dinner paella is usually reheated.
  • ✔ Order paella for minimum 2 people. Single portions are not traditional and often disappointing.
  • ✔ Visit Central Market in the morning for the best atmosphere and fresh products.
  • ✔ Free museum entry on Sundays at La Lonja, Torres, and several others.
  • ✔ Rent a bike to explore Turia Gardens and ride to City of Arts and Sciences (30min).
  • ✔ Beach season: May-October. Water warmest July-September.
  • ✔ Valencia is less touristy than Barcelona/Madrid — locals appreciate efforts to speak Spanish.
  • ✔ Siesta: small shops close 2-5pm, but restaurants and big stores stay open.

🏨 Where to Stay

Best areas for tourists:

  • Ciudad Vella (Old Town): Historic center, walkable to main sights. Can be noisy at night near Plaza del Ayuntamiento.
  • Ruzafa: Trendy neighborhood with excellent restaurants, bars, and vintage shops. Popular with young travelers.
  • El Carmen: Bohemian old town area with street art, nightlife, and authentic atmosphere.
  • Near City of Arts: Modern, quiet area close to beach and futuristic attractions. Less central for old town.
  • Malvarrosa (beach area): Great for beach lovers, but farther from historic center (15min metro).

🎉 Las Fallas Festival

Valencia's biggest event, Las Fallas (March 15-19), is one of Spain's wildest festivals:

  • Giant artistic sculptures (fallas) are displayed throughout the city, then burned on the final night.
  • Daily fireworks (mascletà) at 2pm in Plaza del Ayuntamiento — incredibly loud!
  • Street parties, traditional costumes, paella competitions, and 24-hour celebrations.
  • Book accommodation 6+ months ahead — prices triple during Fallas.
  • Expect massive crowds, noise, and little sleep. Not ideal for quiet travelers or families with small children.

🌅 Day Trip: Albufera

Just 10km south of Valencia, Albufera Natural Park offers a peaceful escape:

  • Take bus 25 from Valencia (45min, €1.50) to El Palmar village.
  • Sunset boat tour on the lagoon (€4-6, 45min) — book at waterfront kiosks.
  • Eat authentic paella in El Palmar restaurants — this is where it originated.
  • Best time: sunset (7-9pm in summer) for stunning views and birdwatching.
