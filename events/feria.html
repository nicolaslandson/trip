🎡 Feria de Abril (Seville Fair)

Overview

The Feria de Abril is one of the most colorful and lively festivals in Spain. Held each spring in Seville, it celebrates Andalusian culture with a week of dancing, music, traditional food, and horse parades. What started as a cattle fair in 1847 has evolved into Seville's biggest annual celebration.

The fairgrounds are filled with over 1,000 temporary tents called casetas, where locals gather to eat, drink, and dance sevillanas — a traditional flamenco-style dance. During the day, visitors can watch beautiful horse-drawn carriages and riders in traditional dress parade through the streets.

At night, the entire fair lights up with thousands of colorful lanterns, music echoing from every caseta, and celebration that lasts until dawn. It's one of the best times to experience authentic local culture in Seville.

🗓️ When & Where

  • Dates: Two weeks after Easter (Semana Santa). Usually late April or early May.
  • Duration: 6 days (Tuesday midnight through Sunday night)
  • Location: Los Remedios district, west of the Guadalquivir River
  • Opening: Monday midnight with the alumbrao (lighting ceremony) — thousands of lights illuminate simultaneously

🏠 Casetas (Tents)

The fair consists of over 1,000 casetas — temporary structures ranging from simple tents to elaborate multi-room pavilions:

  • Private casetas: Owned by families, businesses, or social clubs. Entry by invitation only (most casetas).
  • Public casetas: Open to everyone. Typically run by political parties or the city. Easier for tourists to access.
  • Inside: Tables, bars, dance floors, traditional decorations. Locals dress in traditional Andalusian attire.
  • What to expect: Dancing sevillanas, drinking manzanilla (sherry), eating fried fish and jamón.

Tip: Making friends or joining a tour can help you access private casetas. Otherwise, stick to public ones or enjoy the atmosphere outside.

👗 Traditional Dress

  • Women: Flamenco dresses (trajes de gitana) with ruffles, polka dots, and bright colors. Flowers in hair, shawls, and traditional jewelry.
  • Men: Short jackets (trajes cortos) with tight trousers, wide-brimmed hats, and boots (especially horseback riders).
  • For tourists: Not required to dress up, but many do! Flamenco dresses can be rented in Seville (€30-60/day).

🐴 Horse Parades

One of the most spectacular aspects of Feria de Abril is the afternoon horse parade (paseo de caballos):

  • Hundreds of horses and carriages parade through the fairgrounds
  • Riders in traditional dress, horses decorated with colorful harnesses
  • Elegant carriages carrying families in flamenco dresses
  • Best viewing: 1-5pm daily along the main avenue

🍽️ Food & Drink

  • Pescaíto frito: Fried fish — the classic Feria snack (€8-12)
  • Jamón ibérico: Cured ham (€10-15)
  • Manzanilla: Light, dry sherry from Sanlúcar (€2-3/glass)
  • Rebujito: Manzanilla mixed with lemon soda — the Feria drink (€3-4)
  • Churros con chocolate: For late-night/early-morning snacking (€4-5)

💃 Learning Sevillanas

Sevillanas is THE dance of Feria. It's a four-part flamenco dance everyone knows:

  • Many Sevillanos learn it from childhood
  • Take a quick class before Feria (many studios offer "crash courses" for tourists)
  • YouTube tutorials available — learn the basics in a few hours
  • Don't worry about perfection — enthusiasm matters more than skill!

📝 Practical Tips

  • ✔ Book accommodation 3-6 months ahead — hotels sell out and prices triple
  • ✔ Fair opens around 1pm (after alumbrao night, from noon). Peak time: 10pm-3am
  • ✔ Entrance to fairgrounds is FREE. Only pay inside casetas.
  • ✔ Public casetas charge for food/drinks but anyone can enter
  • ✔ Wear comfortable shoes — you'll walk and dance for hours
  • ✔ Late nights are normal — casetas stay open until 6-7am
  • ✔ Metro and buses run extended hours during Feria
  • ✔ Daytime is family-friendly. Nights get rowdier (but still safe)
  • ✔ Don't confuse with Semana Santa (Holy Week) — that's 2 weeks earlier

🚇 Getting There

  • From city center: Bus C1, C2 to Feria (€1.40)
  • Walking: 30-40min from Cathedral area
  • Taxi: €8-12 from center
  • Best access: San Bernardo metro station + 10min walk
