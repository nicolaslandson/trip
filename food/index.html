Overview

Spanish cuisine is incredibly diverse, varying dramatically from region to region. From the seafood-rich dishes of Galicia to the rice-based paellas of Valencia, from Basque pintxos to Andalusian fried fish, Spain offers one of Europe's most exciting culinary experiences.

Spanish food culture centers around social eating, fresh ingredients, and time-honored traditions. Meals are meant to be enjoyed slowly with friends and family, often late into the evening.

Spanish Meal Times

Understanding when Spanish people eat is crucial for travelers:

  • Breakfast (Desayuno): 7-10am. Usually light: coffee with toast, pastry, or churros. EUR 2-5.
  • Mid-morning snack: 11am-12pm. Common in some regions. Coffee and a small bite.
  • Lunch (Comida): 2-4pm. The main meal. Multi-course, often with wine. This is when to order menu del dia.
  • Merienda (afternoon snack): 5-7pm. Coffee or tea with pastry, especially for children.
  • Dinner (Cena): 9-11pm. Usually lighter than lunch, but restaurants get busiest around 9:30-10:30pm.

Important: Many restaurants close between lunch and dinner (5-8pm). Kitchens may stop taking orders earlier, especially for lunch.

Essential Spanish Dishes

Tapas & Small Plates

  • Patatas bravas: Fried potatoes with spicy tomato sauce and aioli. Found everywhere. EUR 4-6.
  • Jamon iberico: Premium cured ham from acorn-fed pigs. EUR 8-20 depending on quality.
  • Tortilla espanola: Thick potato and egg omelet. Simple but delicious. EUR 3-5 per portion.
  • Croquetas: Creamy fried croquettes with ham, cod, or mushrooms. EUR 1-2 each.
  • Pan con tomate: Toasted bread rubbed with tomato, olive oil, and salt. EUR 2-4.
  • Pulpo a la gallega: Galician-style octopus with paprika and olive oil. EUR 10-15.
  • Pimientos de Padron: Small green peppers fried in olive oil. EUR 4-6.

Rice Dishes

  • Paella valenciana: Traditional with rabbit, chicken, and green beans. Only at lunch. EUR 12-18 per person.
  • Paella de marisco: Seafood paella. EUR 14-20 per person.
  • Arroz negro: Black rice with squid ink and seafood. EUR 12-16.
  • Fideua: Similar to paella but made with noodles. EUR 12-16.

Soups & Stews

  • Gazpacho: Cold tomato soup, perfect for summer. EUR 4-6.
  • Salmorejo: Thicker, creamier version of gazpacho. EUR 5-7.
  • Cocido madrileno: Hearty chickpea and meat stew. EUR 12-18.
  • Fabada asturiana: White bean stew with chorizo and morcilla. EUR 10-14.

Seafood

  • Pescaito frito: Andalusian-style fried fish. EUR 8-12.
  • Gambas al ajillo: Garlic prawns in olive oil. EUR 8-14.
  • Espetos: Sardines grilled over wood fire on the beach. EUR 8-12.
  • Percebes: Gooseneck barnacles. EUR 25-40 per kilo.

Meat Dishes

  • Chuleton: Massive T-bone steak, often sold by weight. EUR 40-60 per kilo.
  • Rabo de toro: Oxtail stew. EUR 12-16.
  • Cochinillo: Roast suckling pig. EUR 18-25.
  • Chorizo al vino: Chorizo sausage cooked in red wine. EUR 5-8.

Regional Specialties

  • Catalonia (Barcelona): Pan con tomate, escalivada, crema catalana.
  • Basque Country: Pintxos, bacalao al pil pil, txangurro.
  • Valencia: Paella, horchata, fartons.
  • Andalusia: Gazpacho, fried fish, salmorejo, flamenquin.
  • Galicia: Pulpo, empanada, percebes, albarino wine.
  • Madrid: Cocido madrileno, callos, huevos rotos.
  • Castilla: Roast lamb, cochinillo, morcilla.

Budget Eating Tips

Menu del Dia

The best budget option in Spain. Available at lunchtime in most restaurants:

  • 3 courses plus bread and a drink
  • Price: EUR 10-15 in local places, EUR 12-18 in tourist areas
  • Usually includes a starter, main, and dessert or coffee
  • Quality ranges from basic to excellent, so check what locals are eating
  • Often written on a chalkboard or separate menu

Free Tapas Cities

In some cities, mainly Granada, Leon, and Salamanca, you get free tapas with every drink:

  • Order beer, wine, or a soft drink for around EUR 2-3
  • Receive a free tapa
  • More drinks means more different tapas
  • You can have a full dinner for around EUR 6-9
  • You do not choose the tapa, the bar decides

Markets

Food markets offer cheap, fresh meals and ingredients:

  • Breakfast: Fresh juice, coffee, tostada. EUR 3-5.
  • Lunch: Market stalls serve simple cooked meals. EUR 6-10.
  • Picnics: Buy cheese, ham, bread, and fruit for beach or park meals.
  • Best markets: Mercado Central in Valencia, La Boqueria in Barcelona, Atarazanas in Malaga.

Supermarket Hacks

  • Ready-made meals from Mercadona or Carrefour: EUR 3-6
  • Fresh bread EUR 0.50-1, good cheese EUR 3-5, quality jamon EUR 5-10
  • Local wine bottles from EUR 2-5
  • Pre-made tortilla espanola: EUR 3-5

Spanish Drinks

Wine

  • Rioja: Famous red wine region. Full-bodied and oak-aged.
  • Ribera del Duero: Premium reds, often pricier than Rioja.
  • Albarino: White wine from Galicia, perfect with seafood.
  • Cava: Spanish sparkling wine from Catalonia.
  • Sherry: Fortified wine from Jerez.
  • House wine: EUR 8-12 per bottle, EUR 2-3 per glass.

Beer

  • Cana: Small draft beer. EUR 1.50-2.50.
  • Doble: Double-sized beer. EUR 2.50-4.
  • Clara: Beer with lemon soda. EUR 2-3.
  • Popular brands: Estrella, Mahou, Cruzcampo, San Miguel.

Other Drinks

  • Sangria: Wine punch with fruit. EUR 10-15 per pitcher.
  • Tinto de verano: Red wine with lemon soda. EUR 2-3.
  • Horchata: Sweet tiger nut drink from Valencia. EUR 2-4.
  • Vermouth: Sunday pre-lunch tradition. EUR 3-4.
  • Cafe con leche: Coffee with milk. EUR 1.50-2.50.

Breakfast & Desserts

Breakfast

  • Churros con chocolate: Fried dough with thick hot chocolate. EUR 4-6.
  • Tostada con tomate: Toast with crushed tomato and olive oil. EUR 2-3.
  • Croissant or napolitana: Pastries with coffee. EUR 2-4.
  • Bocadillo: Sandwich with ham, cheese, or tortilla. EUR 3-5.

Desserts

  • Crema catalana: Catalan custard with burnt sugar top. EUR 4-6.
  • Flan: Caramel custard. EUR 3-5.
  • Tarta de Santiago: Almond cake from Galicia. EUR 4-6.
  • Torrijas: Spanish-style French toast. EUR 4-5.
  • Turron: Almond nougat, especially at Christmas. EUR 5-15 per box.

Dining Etiquette & Tips

  • Do not expect dinner before 9pm.
  • Tipping is not mandatory. Round up or leave EUR 1-2 for good service.
  • Bread is often brought automatically and may be charged if you eat it.
  • Ask for "la cuenta" when you want the bill.
  • Tap water is safe, but ask for "agua del grifo" if you want it.
  • Paella is traditionally a lunch dish, not dinner.
  • Service can be slow. That is part of the local rhythm.

Where to Eat

  • Bar de barrio: Neighborhood bar. Best for menu del dia and authentic tapas.
  • Mercado stalls: Fresh, cheap, local atmosphere. Great for breakfast and lunch.
  • Cerveceria: Beer-focused bar with tapas.
  • Marisqueria: Seafood restaurant. Can be pricey but fresh.
  • Taberna: Traditional tavern with wine and tapas.
  • Restaurante: More formal sit-down option.
  • Chiringuito: Beach bar or restaurant with seafood and paella.

Avoid: Restaurants with photos on the menu, places with touts outside, and anywhere serving paella for dinner.
